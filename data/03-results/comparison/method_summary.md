For 31 October, adding polling and historical information lowers the Republican control probability from 90% in the synthetic-only forecast to 37%. The no-LLM benchmark gives 34%, so including synthetic responses moves the combined forecast toward greater Republican support. Synthetic estimates receive an average weight of 19% in polled states and 17% in unpolled states. The remaining weight goes to state polling averages or, where unavailable, the 2024 CES vote share plus uniform swing. The combined result is therefore driven mainly by these outside estimates. In the comparison without bias correction, we combine the original synthetic shares with polling and historical estimates. The synthetic–poll gap is included in the error estimate, giving synthetic shares an average weight of 11% in polled states. This version gives Republicans a 42% probability of control, compared with 37% under the bias-corrected headline forecast. Both the synthetic shares and their weights differ between these versions.
